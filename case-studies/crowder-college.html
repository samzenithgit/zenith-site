Crowder College economic impact study

Quantifying the regional economic footprint of a Southwest Missouri community college.

A community college and its regional economy

Crowder College, headquartered in Neosho, Missouri, is a public two-year institution serving roughly 5,000 students across multiple campuses in the Four-State region. Like many rural community colleges, Crowder is one of the largest employers in its service area and trains workers for local manufacturing, healthcare, agriculture and skilled trades.

In 2025, Crowder College engaged Zenith Economics to quantify its full economic and fiscal contribution to Southwest Missouri. The college needed figures it could use with legislators, in its capital campaign and in the community.

Our study found that Crowder generates approximately $128 million in total annual economic output and supports roughly 960 jobs across the regional economy, including direct, indirect, and induced effects.

Methodology

Using IMPLAN input-output modeling calibrated to the regional economy of Southwest Missouri, we analyzed five distinct streams of economic activity attributable to Crowder College:

  • Institutional operations. Direct employment, payroll, and procurement spending by the college itself.
  • Capital investment. Construction and capital project spending at Crowder facilities.
  • Student spending. Off-campus living and discretionary spending by Crowder students whose attendance brings them to the region.
  • Visitor spending. Lodging, dining, and retail spending by visitors to the campus for events, athletics, and graduations.
  • Workforce productivity. The increased lifetime earnings of Crowder graduates who remain in the regional workforce.

Each of these streams was modeled separately and aggregated into total impact figures, with industry-specific multipliers for the supply-chain and household-spending effects in Southwest Missouri.

Key findings

The study quantified a return of approximately $2.30 in regional economic activity for every taxpayer dollar invested in Crowder College through state and local appropriations. KSNF/KODE television reported this figure, and the Joplin Globe covered the study. The study also documented how many Crowder graduates go on to work for local manufacturing and healthcare employers.

Crowder College published the results on its economic impact page and announced them in a January 2026 news release that names Zenith Economics as the independent firm that prepared the study.
