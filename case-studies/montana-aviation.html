Montana statewide aviation economic impact study

System-wide analysis of all 75 airports across Montana, conducted for the Montana Department of Transportation.

Every airport in every county

Montana is large and thinly populated, and its airports carry more of the state's travel than in most states. Commercial service at hubs like Bozeman, Billings, and Missoula links the state to the national air network, while general aviation fields in remote counties enable wildfire response, search-and-rescue, agricultural operations, and access to communities that would otherwise be hours from the nearest highway corridor.

The Montana Department of Transportation (MDT) Aeronautics Division engaged our team to produce a system-wide economic impact analysis covering all 75 airports in the state aviation system plan, in partnership with Montana State University. The study supports MDT's grant programs, capital investment planning, and outreach to the state legislature on aviation funding.

Methodology

Each airport in the system received an individualized economic impact estimate, scaled to its operational profile. Commercial-service airports were modeled separately from general-aviation fields, with airline activity, on-airport tenants, and visitor spending each contributing distinct streams to the totals.

We supplemented secondary data with primary research, including surveys of airport managers, tenants, businesses and hospitals, and a survey of more than 7,500 commercial passengers. Visitor spending profiles were segmented by trip purpose and origin, allowing us to distinguish between net new economic activity (out-of-state visitors) and within-state spending displacement.

The analysis follows the FAA framework that aviation planners use, so the results can be compared with other state aviation system studies and cited in federal grant applications.

Categories measured

  • On-airport employment, payroll, and procurement
  • Airline and tenant operations
  • Capital improvements and construction
  • Visitor spending by origin and trip purpose
  • Indirect supply-chain and induced household effects
  • State and local tax revenue contributions

Significance

By covering every airport in every county, the study gives MDT and Montana legislators a county-by-county picture of aviation's economic role. Small rural fields that would never get a study of their own are documented alongside the commercial airports, which helps the case for capital investment at airports that matter to their local economies.

MDT published the 2026 results in a summary report and a technical report, both prepared by Zenith Economics in partnership with Montana State University. Across Montana’s 75 public-use airports, the study found $6.5 billion in total economic output and $2.4 billion in labor income (in 2026 dollars), and more than 39,000 jobs.
