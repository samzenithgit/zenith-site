LACERA pension economic impact study

How the pension benefits LACERA paid in 2025 supported jobs, income and tax revenue in California, Los Angeles County and the county’s districts.

Retiree spending in California

The Los Angeles County Employees Retirement Association (LACERA) administers retirement benefits for Los Angeles County employees. In fiscal year 2025 it paid $4.8 billion in pension benefits. Of its more than 77,000 retirees and survivors, about 63,700 live in California and 42,800 in Los Angeles County.

In 2025 LACERA selected Zenith Economics through a request for proposals to update its 2022 economic impact study. The new study measures the economic and fiscal effects of pension benefit spending in California, Los Angeles County and the county’s five supervisorial districts. LACERA also asked for results for each state legislative and congressional district in the Los Angeles County delegation, which it plans to use in district fact sheets.

Methodology

Pension payments are modeled as household income. Gross benefits are reduced for federal and state income taxes, using Tax Policy Center and NBER TAXSIM effective rates for older households, which leaves about $3.4 billion in disposable income statewide. That income is spent according to IMPLAN’s spending patterns for each household income bracket.

We used a multi-regional IMPLAN model (2024 data year) so that commuting and trade between the county, its districts and the rest of California are captured. Retirees are placed by residential ZIP code and mapped to census tracts and districts with HUD crosswalk files. The study covers pensioner household spending only. It does not include LACERA’s own operating expenses or investment activity, and it does not net out spending that might have happened without the pension.

Los Angeles County tax revenue includes $326.8 million in state and local taxes and $423.2 million in federal revenue. All figures are annual effects of 2025 benefit payments.

District-level reporting

District results let each elected official see the figures for their own constituents. Supervisorial District 5 has the most LACERA pensioners (10,970) and the largest effect, $904.1 million in output and 3,683 jobs. District 3 has the fewest pensioners (4,677) and $379.5 million in output.

We presented the findings to LACERA’s Board of Retirement in June 2026, and LACERA published the report with an announcement on June 11, 2026.
