Real estate & EB-5 economic impact studies

How we help

Real estate development creates significant economic activity during both construction and long-term operations. For developers seeking EB-5 financing, demonstrating job creation that meets USCIS methodological requirements is essential to project viability. For market-rate and affordable housing projects, economic impact data supports zoning applications, community engagement, and public subsidy requests.

We produce EB-5 compliant job creation analyses, development feasibility studies, highest-and-best-use analyses, and general economic impact studies for residential, commercial, industrial, and mixed-use projects of all sizes.

Our method for real estate and EB-5 studies

For EB-5, the test is whether each investor's capital will create at least 10 full-time positions, defined by USCIS as 35 or more hours a week. In a standalone investment only direct jobs at the new commercial enterprise count. Regional center projects may also count indirect and induced jobs estimated with an economically and statistically valid method. Under the EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022, indirect jobs can meet at most 90 percent of each investor's requirement, with tighter limits on jobs from construction lasting less than two years.

We estimate those jobs with RIMS II or IMPLAN. Neither the statute nor the USCIS Policy Manual names a required model, but USCIS reviews whether the multipliers and geographic assumptions are reasonable, so we document the study region and why it fits the project's labor and supplier market. Inputs are hard and soft costs from the business plan, mapped to model sectors, plus projected operating revenue or payroll. Land acquisition is excluded.

Targeted employment area status is decided separately: for petitions filed on or after March 15, 2022, USCIS designates TEAs, applying the statutory rural definition or a census-tract unemployment rate of at least 150 percent of the national average.

Typical inputs we ask for

  • Development budget with hard and soft costs by line item
  • Construction schedule, including start date and total duration
  • Projected operating revenue or payroll after stabilization
  • Site address and parcel information
  • The capital stack, showing the EB-5 share
  • Business plan draft or offering memorandum, so our figures match it

Questions clients ask

Will your job creation report line up with our business plan?

Yes. USCIS reviews whether model inputs such as expenditures and revenues are reasonable, and the business plan is where those figures come from. We reconcile our inputs with the plan's budget, schedule and pro forma before modeling and flag any differences. Your immigration counsel decides how the filing is put together; we provide the economic analysis, not legal advice.

Can you tell us whether the site is in a targeted employment area?

We can prepare the supporting analysis: whether the site falls outside a metropolitan statistical area and any city or town of 20,000 or more, or the weighted unemployment rate of the project tract and contiguous tracts compared with 150 percent of the national average. For petitions filed since March 15, 2022, USCIS makes the designation itself, so our analysis supports the request.

For a housing project, do you count new residents' spending?

Only when the residents are new to the region. Most buyers and renters in a new building move from elsewhere in the same metro area, and their household spending was already happening there, so counting it would overstate the impact. We model construction and building operations, and treat residents' spending as a fiscal question for the city: the taxes they pay set against the services they use.
